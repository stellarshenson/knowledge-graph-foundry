# Completeness, Recall, and Negation in Open-World Knowledge Bases: A Survey

**Authors**: Simon Razniewski, Hiba Arnaout, Shrestha Ghosh, Fabian Suchanek
**Published**: May 2023, arXiv:2305.05403 (journal version of the PVLDB 2021 tutorial)
**Original**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05403
**Local copy**: `[paper] Completeness Recall and Negation in Open-World KBs, 2023-05.pdf`

## Problem

Open-world KBs cannot express what they do NOT know. The survey systematizes a decade of work on making incompleteness explicit and actionable.

## Mechanism taxonomy

Five families of approaches:

1. **Logical foundations** - partial closed-world assumption (PCWA): completeness statements scope where closed-world reasoning is licensed
2. **Statistical estimation** - recall/cardinality estimation per class and property (capture-recapture, cohort prevalence)
3. **Text extraction** - mining cardinality assertions ("has three children") from text to ground expectations
4. **Negative statement identification** - explicit, informative negations as first-class KB content
5. **Relative recall** - completeness measured against comparable entities or KBs rather than absolute truth

Key statistic carried from the AKBC 2016 line: 69-99% of instances in Wikidata/DBpedia/YAGO lack at least one property that sibling entities have.

## Relevance to KGF

The conceptual frame for R04-H23: "the graph knows what it doesn't know" is PCWA operationalized. KGF's gap ledger is the negative-statement family (4) generated by the statistical family (2), consumed twice - as a repair queue at ingest and as an attribute-existence abstention signal at query time (the signal class R03-H17 established that vector similarity cannot provide).
