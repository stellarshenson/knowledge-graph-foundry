**WHEN CAN LLMS ACTUALLY CORRECT THEIR OWN MISTAKES? A CRITICAL SURVEY OF SELF-CORRECTION OF LLMS (2024)**

This TACL survey audits the self-correction literature and finds that most prior claims of success are artifacts of sloppy experimental design. Across the papers it re-examines, the authors report **zero prior studies demonstrate successful self-correction using feedback generated by the same prompted LLM alone**, outside tasks exceptionally suited to it. Self-correction only works reliably when feedback comes from a source external to the generating model - a tool, a retrieval system, or large-scale fine-tuning - not from asking the model to critique itself.

**Key mechanism**
- Defines three research questions (RQ1: can a model self-correct using only its own capability; RQ2: can it self-correct with external information; RQ3: does self-correction beat strong non-correcting baselines) and shows most papers conflate them
- Requires "best-possible initial responses" as the baseline - comparing self-correction against deliberately weakened initial outputs inflates apparent gains
- Categorizes frameworks as Realistic-Fair (deployable, strong initial response), Realistic-Unfair (deployable but weak baseline), and Unrealistic (uses oracle/ground-truth information unavailable at inference)
- Provides an 8-point checklist for designing self-correction experiments that isolate the feedback source's actual contribution

**Main findings**
- RQ1 (self-only feedback): no surveyed paper shows genuine improvement over best-possible initial responses, except narrow tasks where the model can verify its own output cheaply (e.g., constraint satisfaction with checkable structure)
- RQ2 (external feedback): consistently positive - code interpreters, search retrieval, and human feedback all produce reliable self-correction gains when the feedback channel is trustworthy
- RQ3 (fine-tuning): large-scale fine-tuning specifically for correction (not just prompting) enables self-correction even without external tools
- Many influential prior results (e.g., Self-Refine, RCI) target RQ1 but are evaluated as if they answered RQ3, using weak initial baselines that make the correction step look more impactful than it is

**Key takeaways**
- The bottleneck in self-correction is feedback generation, not the refinement step itself - an LLM judging its own output has no new information to exploit
- A verifier must be genuinely external to the generation process (tool execution, retrieval, ground truth, or a separately trained judge) to license a repair loop
- System designs that loop an LLM against its own critique without an external check should expect no reliable gain and risk false confidence from plausible-sounding but ungrounded self-critiques

**Relevance**
- Provides the theoretical license for KGF's repair-at-ingest posture: repair only when a genuinely external verifier exists (schema constraint, graph structural check, source-document lookup) - a same-model "does this extraction look right" critique step is exactly the failure mode this survey documents as non-functional
