**What makes an entity salient in discourse?**

Analyzing **24 spoken and written genres** of English with a graded, summary-worthiness-based operationalization of discourse-level salience, this paper shows that **no single factor determines salience** - multifactorial models combining grammatical, referential, and discourse-structural features are required, and discourse-structural/semantic features consistently outperform morphosyntactic ones (2025).

**Key mechanism**: entity salience is operationalized as graded summary-worthiness across multiple independent summaries (not a binary mention/no-mention label), then regressed against features spanning grammatical function, definiteness, entity type, linear order, discourse relations and hierarchy, referential structure, frequency, and dispersion - with genre as a modulating factor.

**Main findings**: utterance-level prominence predictors (e.g., grammatical subjecthood) do correlate with discourse-level salience, but their effect is modulated by entity-level factors like frequency and dispersion across the document; discourse-structural and semantic features prove more robust predictors than morphosyntactic surface markers; genre and communicative intent drive substantial variation in which features dominate.

**Key takeaways for KGF**: directly undercuts a degree/frequency-only salience prior (H565) - entity prominence is multifactorial, and discourse-structural features (position in discourse hierarchy, referential chains, discourse relations) carry more signal than raw mention count or graph degree. Any salience-weighting scheme for entity materialization or query routing should incorporate discourse-structural signals, not just co-occurrence frequency, and should expect genre-dependent variation.
